## Territory design affects revenue quality
A weak territory model creates confusion everywhere else. Leads sit unassigned, the wrong office follows up, and operators lose trust in the system because routing looks arbitrary.

## The basics that should always be true
- Every opportunity should map to a clear owner or overflow rule
- Borderline areas should have an explicit fallback path
- Response SLAs should differ by territory load and job urgency
- Operators should be able to explain why a lead landed where it did

## Where teams go wrong
Many organizations define territories once and never revisit them. But crew density, service mix, and travel time change over time. The territory map should reflect operating reality, not old assumptions.

## What to watch weekly
- opportunities assigned outside the primary territory
- time-to-first-action by territory
- duplicate ownership conflicts
- booked-job conversion by territory and service line

## The practical takeaway
Good territory management removes politics from routing. When ownership, overflow, and urgency are explicit, the team moves faster and the best opportunities reach the right operator first.
