## The first 24 hours decide the outcome
Catastrophe work does not reward the company that reacts eventually. It rewards the company that already knows its territory coverage, crew constraints, outreach rules, and escalation paths before the event arrives.

## What a real response plan should include
- Territory priority zones and overflow rules
- A clear staffing plan by service line
- Outreach templates approved before the event starts
- A triage model for urgent, review-required, and low-confidence opportunities
- Daily capacity visibility for jobs, inspections, and follow-up

## Why operators get overwhelmed
The failure point is usually not effort. It is context loss. New signals come in faster than the team can qualify them, duplicates pile up, and dispatch starts guessing.

## What strong teams do differently
They keep one operating rhythm:
- verify the signal
- route it to the right territory
- decide the next action fast
- prevent duplicate outreach
- protect technician capacity for the highest-value work

## The practical takeaway
Catastrophe response is an operating system, not a hero moment. If your team cannot see signal quality, territory fit, and next action in seconds, volume will create chaos instead of revenue.
